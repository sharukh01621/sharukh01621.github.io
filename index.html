From First Line to First Paper

Mastering Python, Statistical Data Science, and Applied Deep Learning

A self-contained curriculum written for students, statisticians, and quantitative health researchers. It moves step by step from first Python statements and survey data wrangling to biostatistics, leakage-free Scikit-Learn pipelines, PyTorch architectures (MLPs, CNNs, GRUs, LSTMs, Transformers, LoRA), ablation studies, subgroup fairness audits, Decision Curve Analysis, and TRIPOD+AI reporting.

§ 02

Ongoing research

External overview of active research areas
01
Environmental epidemiology

Environmental exposures and early childhood health

Investigating environmental exposure indicators and early childhood developmental outcomes in nationally representative household surveys through survey-weighted Bayesian modelling and interpretable machine learning.

02
Maternal and child health

Health equity, maternal care, and child nutrition

Examining socioeconomic disparities, maternal healthcare utilization, birth spacing, and child growth patterns across multi-round demographic surveys using multilevel modelling and inequality decomposition.

03
Infectious disease modelling

Climate-sensitive vector-borne disease forecasting

Studying relationships between meteorological variability and infectious disease surveillance dynamics via lagged climate features, statistical time-series forecasting, ensemble trees, and deep sequence architectures.

04
Responsible clinical AI

Fairness-aware and calibrated clinical prediction

Designing probability-calibrated, subgroup-audited machine learning workflows for chronic disease quality-of-life and mental health risk assessment aligned with transparent clinical reporting standards.

05
Field epidemiology

Antimicrobial resistance and community surveys

Building quantitative survey instruments, KoboToolbox and ODK field data workflows, and mixed-methods analytical pipelines for antimicrobial resistance awareness, oral health, and population studies.

06
Open statistical education

Reproducible computational teaching tools

Developing open-source Python packages, automated diagnostic verification engines, and self-contained cloud labs so quantitative health learners can practice reproducible ML without local setup barriers.
